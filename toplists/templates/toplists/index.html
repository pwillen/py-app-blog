The Top 20 Philosophy

A Top 5 list is restrictive. There's enough room for your favorites, but not much room to explore beyond them. A Top 10 starts to open things up, but it can still become a collection of variations on the same preference rather than a representation of someone's broader taste.

A Top 20 gives you room to be a little more individual. It makes space for the unexpected favorites, the unusual choices, and the works that might not immediately come to mind when someone asks what you like.

Think back to when someone asked what kind of music you listened to, and your answer was "all kinds." Maybe you didn't have the language to explain your taste, were embarrassed to share it, or simply hadn't explored yourself enough to understand what you actually liked.

I see a Top 20 as a way to answer that question more meaningfully. Twenty entries give you enough room to communicate something about yourself — not just what you enjoy, but what makes your taste your own.

Why Albums?

I chose albums over individual songs or entire artists because an album is a cohesive piece of work. It's an opportunity to experience an artist's ideas, sound, creativity, and consistency across a complete project.

A great song can make an impression, but an album can establish an identity. The sequencing, atmosphere, recurring themes, and relationship between tracks all contribute to something greater than any one song.

I'm more interested in that lasting artistic value than in identifying a collection of hits. An album gives me something substantial to return to and appreciate as a complete work.
